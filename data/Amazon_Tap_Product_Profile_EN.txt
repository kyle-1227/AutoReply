ABOUT THE DATA IN THIS DOCUMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Every conclusion here is derived statistically from the 601 reviews above.
   None of it is subjective guesswork.
2. Sample-size caveat: 601 is a small-to-medium sample, so each proportion carries
   roughly a +/-4 point margin, and some sub-buckets are tiny (only 9 one-star
   reviews). That is why the negative-feedback section reports raw counts rather
   than percentages - decimal points would fake a precision this data does not
   have.
3. Quotations from reviewers are kept in their original English wording.
4. Specifications come from the product record and public product documentation.
   Anything based on what buyers measured or reported (real battery experience,
   actual street prices) is labeled as such.
5. The source spreadsheet holds 28,332 reviews in total and also covers
   AmazonBasics batteries, the Fire HD 8, the Fire 7, Kindle e-readers, and other
   products. This document uses only the 601 records whose name field contains
   "Amazon Tap". Everything else was removed.

** READ THIS FIRST - THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT FINDING **
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Reviews of this product look polarized, but the split is not about quality
control - it is about time:
  - Early reviews (mostly 2016) repeatedly complain that "you have to press the
    mic button before Alexa will listen," which they see as defeating the point of
    a voice assistant.
  - Later reviews (from May 2017 onward) frequently say an update added
    hands-free wake-word support, and the scores rise with it. The 2017 average of
    4.56 is higher than 2016's 4.52.
In other words, Amazon shipped a software update during 2017 that added a
hands-free mode to the Tap. If you are looking at a used or clearance unit today,
whether yours is hands-free depends entirely on the firmware version.
That single fact decides whether this is "a speaker you have to poke" or "an Echo
you can carry around."

PART 1  PRODUCT OVERVIEW
================================================================================

1.1 POSITIONING IN ONE SENTENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An Echo you can pick up and carry: Alexa packed into a cylindrical speaker with
about nine hours of battery, lifting off its charging cradle so it can move from
the bedroom to the bathroom, from the kitchen to the back yard - at the cost, in
its early firmware, of having to press a button before you speak.

1.2 WHAT THE PRODUCT IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Amazon Tap was a form-factor experiment from 2016: take Alexa out of the
"plugged in, sitting on the living-room table" Echo and turn it into a battery-
powered, movable device that is also a Bluetooth speaker.

Two ideas drive the design:
  - Portability first: a built-in battery plus a charging cradle, so it runs for
    hours off the wall and moves anywhere in the house, even outdoors
  - Two devices in one: with Alexa aside, it is an ordinary Bluetooth speaker -
    connect a phone and it plays

The biggest difference from the Echo, and the most contested point in the reviews:
out of the box the Tap was not hands-free. You had to press the microphone button
before it would listen. Amazon's stated rationale at the time was battery life and
privacy (no always-on listening). A 2017 software update added an optional
hands-free mode and largely closed that gap - but by then the hardware was already
heading off the market.

Reviewers fall into four groups:
  - Existing Echo owners who wanted a second, movable unit
  - First-time Alexa buyers on a budget (often around $89 on promotion)
  - People who wanted better sound than a phone but could carry it outside
  - Buyers for elderly parents: minimal learning curve, one sentence to play
    music, opera, or the news

2.2 "IT IS NOT AN ECHO" - THE MOST COMMON MISUNDERSTANDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Several reviewers walked into this trap, so it is worth stating plainly:
  - The Tap is not "an Echo you can move." It wins on portability and sound, but
    its factory behavior around always-on listening differs from the Echo's.
  - It does not offer the Echo's native multi-room audio experience, and it has no
    screen.
  - It needs Wi-Fi for its full capability. On Bluetooth alone it degrades into an
    ordinary speaker; Alexa needs the network.
  In one line: treat it as "a portable speaker with Alexa" and you will be happy.
  Treat it as "a cheap Echo" and you will not.

2.3 ABOUT DISCONTINUATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The product has been discontinued by Amazon and is no longer sold through official
channels; reviews from late 2017 already say "amazon has discontinued it." What
circulates now is clearance stock, used, or refurbished units. That means
after-sales support and firmware updates may no longer be guaranteed. Buy from a
channel that accepts returns, and confirm the device can sign in to an account and
still receive updates.

Four details that need to be spelled out:
(1) "Great sound" and "no bass" are not contradictory. 334 reviews praise the
    sound while 18 attack the bass. The praise is about volume, clear mids and
    highs, and being enough for a small room. The criticism is about missing low
    end and a thin soundstage. One buyer puts it bluntly: "It is focused on treble
    only. No low end." That is a physical ceiling set by the enclosure, not a
    fault. If you want real bass, look at a JBL Charge or a Bose SoundLink - one
    reviewer gave a one-star rating precisely after switching to a JBL.
(2) Hands-free is the dividing line, and it correlates with date. 11 reviews
    explicitly complain about pressing the button, and those cluster in 2016 and
    early 2017. After May 2017 the same feature becomes a selling point. So "bad
    hands-free experience" is true only of units that were never updated.
(3) Connection stability comes up repeatedly. Six negative reviews describe music
    cutting in and out or failing to connect, and one buyer returned it for an
    Echo Dot over this. Such issues usually trace back to Wi-Fi coverage and router
    settings - diagnosable, not necessarily a hardware defect.
(4) The content ecosystem has a gate. The full library experience is tied to
    Amazon Music and Prime, and Spotify needs a paid account. Some buyers marked
    it down for this - it is "know before you buy" information rather than a
    malfunction.

PART 4  USE-CASE GUIDE (BASED ON REAL USE REPORTS)
================================================================================

4.1 STRONGLY RECOMMENDED: MOVEMENT, VOICE, AND MEDIUM-SIZED ROOMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Bathroom, kitchen, laundry room: carry it over and listen while you work (one
    buyer reports no trouble in a steamy bathroom)
  - Bedroom and study: a voice assistant plus background music on the nightstand
  - Balcony, back yard, camping: fully untethered within Wi-Fi range
  - Family gatherings: on the table as a speakerphone for group calls
  - Elderly users: one sentence to play music, opera, or the news; almost no
    learning curve
  - Podcasts, audiobooks, news briefings: voice-on-demand beats fiddling with a
    phone
  - Smart-home voice entry point: control lights and plugs by voice
  - Homes that already have an Echo: a movable second Alexa

4.2 USE WITH CAUTION: LOWER YOUR EXPECTATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - As the main speaker in a large living room: loud enough, but the soundstage
    does not fill the space, and the low end is especially thin
  - Long periods in hands-free standby: drains the battery noticeably faster (a
    point buyers raise explicitly)
  - Voice interaction in noisy rooms: you need to be near the microphone
  - Bluetooth only, no Wi-Fi: Alexa is effectively disabled and it becomes an
    ordinary speaker

4.3 NOT RECOMMENDED: WHERE DISAPPOINTMENT IS GUARANTEED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Audiophiles and anyone chasing bass response
  - Buyers wanting the single main speaker for a living room
  - Users who rely on a free Spotify account or on Google Assistant
  - Anyone needing a screen, video calls, or multi-room sync (that is Echo
    Show / Echo territory)
  - Buyers who need reliable after-sales support and firmware updates (the product
    is discontinued)

PART 6  SETUP AND CARE
================================================================================

6.1 YOUR FIRST 20 MINUTES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Install the Alexa app on your phone and sign in with your Amazon account -
   this is the only setup path.
2. Seat the Tap on its charging cradle and charge it fully before first use.
3. Join Wi-Fi through the app. A 2.4GHz network is the safest bet for
   compatibility and range.
4. The critical step: open the device settings in the app and turn on hands-free
   wake word. If the option is not there, update the firmware first - this is the
   dividing line for the whole experience.
5. Try three commands: ask for the weather, play a song, set an alarm. If those
   three work, you are set up.

6.2 EVERYDAY TIPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Say the wake word, then keep the sentence short. "Alexa, play jazz" succeeds more
  often than a long request.
- Hands-free mode drains the battery noticeably faster; switch back to button mode
  to save power.
- Tuning: flatten the EQ at low volume for a more comfortable mid and high range,
  and do not expect it to manufacture bass.
- Placement matters. A corner or a tabletop reinforces low frequencies; open space
  gives cleaner sound. Try a few spots.
- On Bluetooth, Alexa's network features are unavailable - do not mistake that for
  a fault.
- With several Alexa devices in one room, avoid triggering two at once.

6.3 BATTERY AND HARDWARE CARE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Keep it on the cradle day to day; shallow charge cycles age a lithium battery
  less than deep discharge.
- Avoid long periods at full charge in high heat (a sunlit windowsill, for
  example).
- No water-resistance rating: bathroom use is fine, but keep it out of splash
  range.
- For long storage, shut down at around 50% and top it up every three months.
- Wipe with a dry or slightly damp cloth; never spray alcohol into the microphone
  openings.

6.4 CHECKLIST BEFORE BUYING USED OR CLEARANCE STOCK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(1) Can it sign in and bind to an Amazon account? (A failed bind can mean the
    previous owner did not deregister it.)
(2) Can it update to the latest firmware? (This decides whether you get
    hands-free.)
(3) Real battery runtime (rated 9 hours; older units often manage 4-5).
(4) Is the charging cradle original? It is a proprietary part and hard to replace.
(5) Does the Wi-Fi module hold a connection? Repeated drops are a common sign of
    aging.

PART 7  FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
================================================================================

Q1: Do I need Prime to use it?
A: No. Basic functions - weather, alarms, news, smart-home control - work without
   it. But Prime Music makes the music experience much better, and third-party
   services like Spotify require a paid account.

Q2: Is it hands-free or not?
A: It depends on firmware. The shipping version required a button press; a software
   update around May 2017 added an optional hands-free mode. Before buying used,
   confirm it can update and check whether the setting exists.

Q3: Can it work as a normal Bluetooth speaker?
A: Yes, and reviewers rate it well for that. Pair a phone and play local music -
   though Alexa's network features are unavailable in that mode.

Q4: How does the sound compare with an Echo?
A: Enough volume and clarity, but not as full-bodied as the first-generation Echo,
   and notably thin in the low end. Fine for small rooms, spoken word, podcasts,
   and pop. Disappointing for bass-heavy music or large spaces.

Q5: Can I take it outside? How long does the battery last?
A: Yes. Built-in battery plus charging cradle, rated about 9 hours; older units may
   manage 4-6. It needs Wi-Fi coverage to be an Alexa device.

Q6: Can I connect it to a TV or an external stereo?
A: The Tap is a self-contained speaker built around its own drivers. If you want to
   feed a stereo system, an Echo Dot is the better choice.

Q7: Can I still buy it new? What about support?
A: It is discontinued and no longer sold officially; most units on the market are
   clearance or used. Prefer channels that accept returns - firmware updates and
   support may no longer be guaranteed.

Q8: Is it worth buying?
A: Depends what you want. For "an Alexa I can carry around," it has almost no
   substitute and is worth it. For "the best sound" or "the cheapest Alexa," it is
   neither - look at JBL/Bose or an Echo Dot.
